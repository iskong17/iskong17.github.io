I spent Christmas Eve 2024 in Fukuoka, walking between Tenjin and Hakata from lunch until late in the evening. The city is small enough to cover on foot, and most of the day followed the water: the canals, the Naka River, and the stream that runs through Canal City.

## Lunch: Hakata ramen at Shin-Shin

The day started at the main store of **Shin-Shin** in Tenjin, one of the best-known names for Hakata ramen. The blue sign with the chef holding a bowl is hard to miss.

![The main store of Hakata ramen Shin-Shin in Tenjin](/images/blog/fukuoka-2024/shinshin-ramen-shop.jpg)

Hakata ramen is the tonkotsu style the city is known for: a creamy pork-bone broth with thin, straight noodles. My bowl came with chashu, wood-ear mushrooms, plenty of green onion and two soft-boiled eggs. The plate behind it is *kaedama*, an extra serving of noodles you drop into the leftover soup, which is how Hakata ramen is meant to be eaten.

![A bowl of tonkotsu ramen with eggs and an extra serving of noodles](/images/blog/fukuoka-2024/hakata-ramen.jpg)

## An afternoon along the canal

After lunch I walked toward Hakata. The canals between the two districts are quiet in the afternoon, with apartment blocks on both banks and the buildings reflected in the still water.

![A quiet canal between apartment buildings on a clear afternoon](/images/blog/fukuoka-2024/canal-daytime.jpg)

## Canal City at Christmas

By the evening I reached **Canal City Hakata**, a shopping complex built around an artificial canal. For Christmas, the trees, balconies and the water itself were covered in lights, and the open stage in the middle was crowded.

![Canal City Hakata lit up for Christmas](/images/blog/fukuoka-2024/canal-city-illumination.jpg)

Just outside, the bank of the **Naka River** had its own row of lit trees. From the bridge you can see the Grand Hyatt, Canal City and the lights reflected in the river.

![The Naka River at dusk with the Grand Hyatt and Canal City](/images/blog/fukuoka-2024/nakagawa-night.jpg)

## Dinner: Hakata oden

The day ended at a small counter serving **Hakata oden**. The menu covered a whole wall, and the bowl I ordered had daikon, a fried fish cake, konjac and beef tendon on a skewer, all simmered in a light dashi broth.

![A bowl of Hakata oden with daikon, a fish cake, konjac and beef tendon](/images/blog/fukuoka-2024/oden.jpg)
